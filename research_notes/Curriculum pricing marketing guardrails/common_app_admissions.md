# Common App and study-abroad application support: market and product facts (as of 7 Nov 2026)

Method note: about 17 tool calls. Search results contained many SEO or content-farm pages (pages.dev, vercel.app, blogspot). I did not cite those. Several official pages (UCAS, NACAC, IECA, Common App ethics policy) were not retrievable or did not show the requested content, so those items are listed under Gaps. Items labelled "background knowledge" are not source-verified in this session and must be checked before publishing.

## 1. Common App: how it works, monetisation, scale, international and Bangladesh data, third-party and AI rules

### Takeaway
Common App is large (about 1,146 members, 1.53M first-year applicants in 2025-26) and the 2026-27 essay prompts are unchanged. International applicants fell 10% in 2025-26, and Bangladesh is still a top-15 non-US source country. Official sources I could fetch said nothing on third-party consultants or AI essay policy. Treat those rules as unverified until the Common App terms and NACAC/IECA pages are checked directly.

### Cited Findings
- 2025-26 first-year applicants were 1,527,328 (+2%). Applications were 10,786,782 (+6%), and applications per applicant were 7.06. — [Common App End of Season Report 25-26](https://www.commonapp.org/files/DAR/deadline-updates/2025-26/Common-App-End-of-Season-Report_25-26.pdf)
- Member institutions in 2025-26 were 1,146 (up from 1,097): 363 public and 714 private. — [same report](https://www.commonapp.org/files/DAR/deadline-updates/2025-26/Common-App-End-of-Season-Report_25-26.pdf)
- The Common App deck reports 1.1K+ members, 60+ international members, and 600+ members that charge no application fee. — [What's New with Common App 2026-27](https://commonapp.org/files/webinar_decks/Whats-New-with-Common-App-First-Year-2026-2027.pdf)
- International applicants fell 10% overall in 2025-26 (Asia -11%, India -15%, Africa -17%, Americas +5%). Kenya was the only top-ten international country to grow (+29%). The report gives no absolute international count or Bangladesh figure. — [End of Season Report](https://www.commonapp.org/files/DAR/deadline-updates/2025-26/Common-App-End-of-Season-Report_25-26.pdf)
- Common App's own deck lists about 10% of 2025-26 applicants as international, from 210+ countries and territories. The top non-US countries listed were China, India, Ghana, Canada, Pakistan, Nigeria, Uzbekistan, UAE, Kenya, Ethiopia, Saudi Arabia, Bangladesh (12th), Kazakhstan, Brazil, South Korea. The order appears to be rank order, but the deck does not say so explicitly. — [What's New 2026-27](https://commonapp.org/files/webinar_decks/Whats-New-with-Common-App-First-Year-2026-2027.pdf)
- Essay prompts for 2026-27 are unchanged from the prior year. Common App announced them on 27 Feb 2026, and the 2026-27 application opened 31 Jul / 1 Aug 2026. — [What's New deck](https://commonapp.org/files/webinar_decks/Whats-New-with-Common-App-First-Year-2026-2027.pdf); [Common App Direct Admissions blog](https://www.commonapp.org/blog/common-app-expands-direct-admissions-program-2026-27-234-participating-institutions)
- 2026-27 product changes:
  - The Activities section is renamed "Activities and experiences", the position/leadership field is optional, and character limits are unchanged.
  - Email verification is required for new accounts.
  - Counselors can add a text midyear report.
  - Duolingo, PTE and TOEFL score fields were updated.
  - Citizenship wording was clarified.
  - Independent-learner logic and a first-generation question were added.
  — [What's New deck](https://commonapp.org/files/webinar_decks/Whats-New-with-Common-App-First-Year-2026-2027.pdf)
- Fee waivers: colleges that charge a fee now state whether they accept the Common App fee waiver for all students, US students only, or none. This is shown in the fee waiver question, on college pages and in review-and-submit. — [What's New deck](https://commonapp.org/files/webinar_decks/Whats-New-with-Common-App-First-Year-2026-2027.pdf)
- International students may qualify for the need-based fee waiver only if the college accepts it, and some colleges have their own waivers. — [Common App for International (PDF)](https://www.commonapp.org/static/67884e7e31b6083ad9952a7f8a913ec0/Common-App-for-International_0.pdf)
- Recommenders and school forms: counselors submit transcripts and school forms. If there is no counselor, a school official with access to academic records can be invited. Recommenders use Common App's system or a partner. Requirements that vary by college include English-proficiency tests, non-English transcripts, fee-waiver acceptance and financial aid for internationals. — [Common App for International](https://www.commonapp.org/static/67884e7e31b6083ad9952a7f8a913ec0/Common-App-for-International_0.pdf)
- Test-optional trend: applicants reporting a test score grew 11% in 2025-26, and non-reporters fell 5%. Reporters outnumbered non-reporters for the first time since 2019-20. Only 5% of members required a score in 2025-26. — [End of Season Report](https://www.commonapp.org/files/DAR/deadline-updates/2025-26/Common-App-End-of-Season-Report_25-26.pdf)
- Direct Admissions: announced 25 Aug 2026 with 234 institutions. Proactive offers go to first-generation and low-to-middle-income students. The page does not say whether international students are eligible. — [Common App blog](https://www.commonapp.org/blog/common-app-expands-direct-admissions-program-2026-27-234-participating-institutions)
- The 2025-26 first-year application closed 28 Jul 2026. — [What's New deck](https://commonapp.org/files/webinar_decks/Whats-New-with-Common-App-First-Year-2026-2027.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 650-word limit, the 7 prompts (including "topic of your choice"), the 10 activity slots, 5 honors and the Additional Information section are background knowledge. The user's brief also states them. Verify against commonapp.org before the product copy goes live. I did not retrieve the 2026-27 prompt text.
- Common App monetises through member dues and fees paid by colleges, not students. Applicants pay application fees to colleges (not to Common App), and 600+ members charge no fee. This is background knowledge and is not confirmed by a fetched source.
- Bangladesh is a meaningful but not top-tier source (12th among non-US countries listed). Falling international applications overall suggest the Bangladeshi cohort is also under visa and cost pressure, but no Bangladesh-specific trend was found.
- Since Common App is free for students at no-fee members, a paid product has to sell guidance and quality rather than access.

### Gaps
- Common App official 2026-27 essay prompt text and word limits: not retrieved. Search results were third-party blogs only.
- Number of Bangladeshi applicants and Bangladesh trend: not in any source found.
- ED/EA/RD deadline dates and the share of members offering each plan: the report text excludes these; deadlines are college-specific.
- Common App policy on third-party consultants, the applicant "Affirmation" about authorship and the fraud policy, AI-use rules, and NACAC/IECA code-of-ethics wording: not retrieved. Search turned up only blog commentary.
- Whether a paid counselor can be listed in the Common App: unverified. Background knowledge says the school counselor and recommenders are school-based, and there is no standard field for independent consultants. Confirm in the Common App Help Center.

## 2. UCAS (UK) personal statement and graduate admissions norms

### Takeaway
UCAS replaced the free-form statement with three guided questions for 2026 entry. The UCAS page I retrieved still mentions a 4,000-character total. Fees, deadlines and graduate norms were not verified from primary sources.

### Cited Findings
- The three questions: "Why do you want to study this subject or subjects at degree level?", "How have you prepared yourself for this course or courses?", and "What else have you done to prepare outside of education, and why are these experiences useful?" Suggested lengths were about 150, 250 and 100 words. — [Leading Tuition guide](https://www.leadingtuition.co.uk/blog/ucas-personal-statement-2026-2027-three-questions-complete-guide) (secondary source)
- The UCAS writing page refers to "4,000 characters" in total. It did not show the three-question structure in the fetch. — [UCAS: writing a personal statement](https://www.ucas.com/undergraduate/applying-university/writing-personal-statement)
- Most UCAS deadlines for 2026 entry fall in January 2027, and UCAS opened in September 2026. — [Leading Tuition](https://www.leadingtuition.co.uk/blog/ucas-personal-statement-2026-2027-three-questions-complete-guide) (secondary; this page describes 2026 entry, so confirm 2027-entry dates)

### Inferences
- Because the format is structured, a guided "answer each question" feedback tool fits well, and the UK is a good second market after the US.
- The ability to reuse one UK statement across five choices is background knowledge; verify.

### Gaps
- UCAS fee for 2027 entry, exact 2027-entry deadlines (15 Jan equal consideration, 30 Jun late, Oxbridge/medicine in October), and international-specific guidance: not retrieved.
- GRE requirement trend, SOP/CV/LOR norms by country: not researched (call budget). Do not state specifics without sourcing.
- Canada, Australia, Germany and Malaysia application mechanics: not researched.

## 3. Commercial models of admissions-support companies and agent economics

### Takeaway
Agent revenue in the UK, Australia and Canada is mostly university commission. The only retrieved figure is Australia at about 11-17% of first-year tuition. For a Bangladesh-based platform, the ethical and regulatory pressure points are commissions, ghostwriting and outcome promises.

### Cited Findings
- Australian universities typically pay agents 11-17% of first-year tuition, about A$5,000-10,000 per student, with lower repeat-year payments. UNSW paid A$133.3M in commissions in 2024 (A$78M in 2023). Curtin paid A$52.4M and JCU A$39.4M. The sector total was over A$530M. The article notes inconsistencies in its own ranges, so treat these as approximate. — [AcademicJobs](https://www.academicjobs.com/au/higher-education-news/australian-universities-agent-commissions-unsw-dollar133m-or-academicjobs-5921) (secondary, no named primary source for sector totals)
- Commissions for onshore transfers are banned from 31 Mar 2026. — [same](https://www.academicjobs.com/au/higher-education-news/australian-universities-agent-commissions-unsw-dollar133m-or-academicjobs-5921)
- A Bangladesh competitor in the same category: 10 Minute School's study-abroad service offers 1:1 counselling, shortlisting, CV/SOP/LOR help, visa interview prep, scholarship and loan guidance. It claims "No File Opening Fee" and a free initial consultation, but the page gives no prices. Its scholarship table appears to contain errors (e.g. Chevening described as "mostly for Americans"). — [10 Minute School](https://10minuteschool.com/en/product/study-abroad/)
- The page for IDP Bangladesh advertises free study-abroad counselling, which reflects the commission-funded "free to student" model. — [IDP Bangladesh](https://idp.com/bangladesh/what-we-do/free-study-abroad-counselling) (search result listing only; page not fetched)

### Inferences
- A "free to the student, paid by university" model is already standard in Bangladesh (IDP, 10 Minute School and others). A test-prep platform could compete on a paid, transparent, student-aligned model for US and graduate applications, where commissions are minimal.
- The ethical line for the product is: coach, structure, give feedback and review, but do not write essays or SOPs. Ghostwriting risks student misconduct findings and visa-refusal consequences; no source was retrieved on this, so it is a reasoned judgement.

### Gaps
- Pricing and models for CollegeVine, Crimson Education, Yocket, Leverage Edu, Shemmassian and PrepScholar: not retrieved.
- BDT fee ranges for Bangladeshi consultancies (counselling, SOP writing): none found; the Bangladeshi pages that appeared do not publish prices.
- UK and Canadian commission percentages: not found from a reliable source.
- ICEF agent certification, UK Agent Quality Framework, British Council training: not researched. UK and Canadian commission rates should not be asserted.

## 5. Visa context 2025-2026

### Takeaway
Refusal rates for Bangladeshi students are high for both the US (73%) and Australia (51% offshore higher-education refusals in Feb 2026), which supports demand for visa-interview and financial-documentation help but also raises the risk of overpromising.

### Cited Findings
- US F-1 refusal rate in 2025: Bangladesh 73%, Pakistan 71%, India 61% (53% in 2024), Nepal 81%. The global rate was 35% (31% in 2024). The data are attributed to Shorelight analysis. — [ICEF Monitor, 15 Apr 2026](https://monitor.icef.com/2026/04/visa-rejections-climb-in-the-us-for-international-students-from-key-markets-including-india/pexels-stephen-westby-295716816-13327170-600/)
- Australia: 51% of offshore higher-education visa applications from Bangladesh were refused in Feb 2026, while applications from Bangladesh rose 51% year on year. The overall offshore approval rate was 67.6%, the lowest monthly level in at least 21 years. Data from Home Affairs, published 6 Apr 2026 and reported by Times Higher Education. — [The Daily Star](https://online-d11.thedailystar.net/campus/news/australian-student-visa-rejections-hit-record-high-alarming-bangladeshi-applicants-4145701)

### Inferences
- High refusal rates in the US and Australia may push demand toward Germany, Malaysia and the UK, but I found no data on this shift.

### Gaps
- US social-media vetting rules and dates, UK dependants ban details, Canada study-permit caps for 2026: not verified. Brief background knowledge only: the UK dependants restriction began in January 2024, and Canada introduced caps in 2024. Verify and date these before use.
- Whether the 73% US figure is for F-1 only and how it was calculated: the ICEF excerpt attributes it to Shorelight. A different tracker (VisaGrader) was listed in search but not fetched.

## 6. Productisable tools

### Takeaway
Based on the evidence, the strongest-fit tools are structured, process-oriented ones. Each of these rests on reasoning, not on a sourced study.

### Cited Findings
- Common App requires structured inputs per college (fee waiver acceptance, English test requirements, non-English transcript policy, international aid). — [Common App for International](https://www.commonapp.org/static/67884e7e31b6083ad9952a7f8a913ec0/Common-App-for-International_0.pdf)
- The UCAS statement now has three guided questions. — [Leading Tuition](https://www.leadingtuition.co.uk/blog/ucas-personal-statement-2026-2027-three-questions-complete-guide)

### Inferences
- Deadline tracker and document checklist: per-college requirements vary, and the data model is straightforward. High value, low ethical risk.
- College list builder: pair with need-aware/need-blind and international-aid flags, since only a few colleges are need-blind for internationals.
- Essay feedback (not writing): structured rubric feedback with a rule that the tool never generates essay text for the student. This avoids misconduct risk, though it needs checking against Common App's affirmation and college AI policies.
- Chancing estimates: label as indicative only, and avoid precise percentages, since the data are noisy and test-optional trends complicate the picture.
- Interview and visa-interview prep: demand is supported by the high refusal rates above, but do not promise outcomes.
- Scholarship finder: only with data from official provider pages and clear deadline dates, because competitor pages contained errors.

### Gaps
- No competitor usage or willingness-to-pay data was found.
