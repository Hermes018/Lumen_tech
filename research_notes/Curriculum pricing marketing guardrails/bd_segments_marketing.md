# Bangladeshi learner segments and marketing English-test prep (as of 2026-11-07)

Scope note: research was capped at ~20 tool calls. Several key questions (Q3 channels, Q4 income/affordability, Q6 international) are only thinly covered; gaps are listed explicitly. Nothing below is from memory unless in Gaps as "unverified".

## 1. Demand drivers and tests actually needed per segment

### Takeaway
UK skilled/health-and-care routes now require B2 (first applications from 8 Jan 2026) and overseas care-worker recruitment closed 22 July 2025, so the "middle-aged care/skilled worker via IELTS" pitch is weakened for UK. Low-income Gulf/Asia migrants mostly need destination-language tests (BMET says a mandatory language test applies to Korea, Japan, Hong Kong), not IELTS. Fraud is a live, well-reported problem in BD, so trust signals matter.

### Cited Findings
- UK: from 22 July 2025 employers can no longer sponsor new care workers from abroad; those already in the UK may stay for their visa duration — [Gherson](https://www.gherson.com/blog/ending-overseas-recruitment-for-care-worker-visas/)
- UK: B2 English applies to Skilled Worker (incl. Health and Care), Scale-up and High Potential Individual first-time applications submitted on/after 8 January 2026; before that B1 applied; people already holding Skilled Worker permission are not automatically required to meet B2 on re-application — [Davidson Morris](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/b2-english-test/) (law-firm marketing page; confirm on gov.uk)
- UK family routes "continue to operate staged English language thresholds"; this source does not state the levels or Life Skills status — [Davidson Morris](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/b2-english-test/)
- IDP Bangladesh fee page lists IELTS Life Skills (A1 and B1) at BDT 24,750, so Life Skills is still sold in BD (page undated, title says 2026) — [IDP Bangladesh](https://ielts.idp.com/bangladesh/about/ielts-exam-fee)
- BMET: mandatory language test required for labour migration to South Korea, Japan and Hong Kong; Japan, Korea, Germany show strong demand with language often mandatory for skilled/semi-skilled jobs; Gulf employers increasingly value communication skills — [Financial Express](https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/print/third-language-push-aims-to-boost-global-job-prospects-1772215386)
- A 2023 ministry study of 479 mostly Gulf migrant workers: 52% saw language barriers as a major challenge (small sample) — same Financial Express article
- Japan Foundation JFT-Basic is the test for Japan SSW; Bangla info page exists — [JPF](https://www.jpf.go.jp/jft-basic/bn/about/index.html)
- Fraud news: arrests over fake IELTS question papers ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/amp/bangladesh/390928/2-arrested-for-defrauding-students-with-fake-ielts)); IELTS "leaked papers" mismatch/arrests ([Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/national/leaked-ielts-tests-papers-dont-match-police-complaint-leads-to-arrests)); education consultancy owner arrested over Tk 15.5 crore overseas study scam ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/crime/overseas-education-scam-just-thought-consultancy-owner-arrested-while-trying-leave)); visa fraud ring arrests ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/crime/2-members-visa-fraud-ring-arrested-sirajganj-674042)); certificate forgery arrest ([Daily Star, Apr 2024](https://images.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/crime-justice/news/certificate-forgery-computer-operator-held-dhaka-3595736?amp)). I only read headlines/snippets, not full articles.
- Global IELTS leak syndicate item from a Sydney local outlet — [Suprovat Sydney](https://suprovatsydney.com.au/?p=12006) (weak source, unverified)

### Inferences
- Segment (b) UK care-worker demand is largely closed for new overseas entrants; Canada/Australia/NZ/Ireland, nursing (OET/IELTS) and spouse routes are the remaining adult-migrant prompts but are unverified here.
- Segment (c) should be sold language/skill readiness for Korea (EPS-TOPIK), Japan (JFT-Basic/JLPT), Gulf spoken Arabic/English, not IELTS; IELTS marketing to this segment risks mis-targeting.
- "Official-source verified", "no leaked papers", refund and transparent-pricing messaging are justified by the fraud environment.

### Gaps
- Not verified: UK spouse/partner level (A1/A2/B1 and whether Life Skills remains acceptable post-2026), Canada CLB thresholds, Australia, NZ, Ireland, NMC/OET requirements, Malaysia/Europe study requirements. Not searched.
- EPS-TOPIK specifics and current Bangladesh–Korea intake status not confirmed.
- No full-text read of fraud articles; no data on prevalence.

## 2. Bangladeshi digital behaviour by segment

### Takeaway
Early 2026 data: 47% internet penetration (82.8M users), 57.8% of population rural, and a large gender skew on social platforms. Women are far behind on smartphones and mobile internet, with data cost and digital skills as top barriers.

### Cited Findings
- Population 176M (57.8% rural); 186M mobile connections; 82.8M internet users = 47.0%; 93.4M offline; median mobile download 36.84 Mbps (Ookla, Aug 2025) — [DataReportal Digital 2026 Bangladesh](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-bangladesh)
- Ad reach: Facebook 64.0M (36.9% female / 63.1% male); YouTube 49.8M (adult audience 35.9% female); TikTok 56.2M among adults 18+ (29.9% female); ad reach is not MAU, YoY growth figures should be treated cautiously — same DataReportal page
- GSMA Mobile Gender Gap Report 2024 (via Daily Star, May 2024): mobile ownership 85% men vs 68% women; mobile internet 40% vs 24%; smartphone ownership 40% vs 22%; data cost the leading individual barrier (24% women, 15% men); handset cost and digital skills top barriers — [Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/women-still-lag-mobile-ownership-internet-adoption-3613001)
- MFS: 239.3M registered accounts at Jan 2025 (up 9.2% YoY); monthly transactions about Tk 1.72 trillion — [Financial Express, Mar 2025](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/bangladesh-mfs-accounts-surge-by-20-million-in-a-year-transactions-up-32pc). Registered accounts are not unique or active users.
- Another Financial Express piece notes MFS growth slowing with data gaps — [Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/economy/bangladesh/mfs-growth-slows-amid-data-gaps-dip-in-remittances) (headline only)

### Inferences
- Women in segment (b) are reachable mostly via shared/family phones; WhatsApp/IMO/Facebook voice-first and low-data formats suit better than app-heavy ones.
- Facebook/TikTok are male-skewed in measured reach, so women-targeted campaigns need creator/community channels and family-decision-maker messaging.

### Gaps
- No rural vs urban split of smartphone/internet use found; no WhatsApp/IMO usage figures; no operator social-pack prices (GP/Robi/Banglalink); no Bangla-content preference data; no bKash/Nagad split or rural agent counts.

## 3. Marketing channels and tactics

### Takeaway
Little citable evidence gathered. One price-point reference (Mentors) shows seasonal/deadline discount tactics are normal.

### Cited Findings
- Mentors offers bundles with listed vs discounted prices and a validity date of 15 Nov 2026 (e.g., IELTS Basic Package BDT 19,900 vs listed 29,440) — [Mentors](https://www.mentors.com.bd/course-fee)
- 10 Minute School has an English-medium and Bangla blog/product presence ([blog](https://blog.10minuteschool.com/?p=74683)); my search returned no usable data on its channel strategy.

### Inferences
- Crossed-out "listed" prices with expiry dates are a norm competitors use; trust-focused brands may differentiate with transparent pricing (see Q5).

### Gaps
- Not found: 10 Minute School strategy, creator/influencer rates, Facebook group tactics, UDC, BMET TTC, Probashi Kallyan Bank, BRAC Migration, seasonality (HSC results, Ramadan/Eid), SMS marketing results.

## 4. Pricing and affordability

### Takeaway
Test fees alone are large relative to low incomes: IELTS on computer BDT 31,100. Coaching runs roughly BDT 9,500–19,900 at one prominent centre.

### Cited Findings
- IDP Bangladesh fees (undated page, titled 2026): IELTS on Computer BDT 31,100; UKVI BDT 34,850; Life Skills A1/B1 BDT 24,750; one-skill retake BDT 18,600 (UKVI 20,900). The page did not list separate Academic/General Training fees — [IDP Bangladesh](https://ielts.idp.com/bangladesh/about/ielts-exam-fee)
- Mentors on-campus: IELTS Regular BDT 13,800 (3.5 months, 20 mocks); Crash BDT 9,500 (1.5 months); Executive/Weekend BDT 13,800; Basic Package BDT 19,900; SAT+IELTS bundles BDT 19,000–22,500; no installment info on page — [Mentors](https://www.mentors.com.bd/course-fee)
- Third-party blog guides on IELTS cost exist ([BanglaYIELTS](https://banglayielts.com/?p=45629)) — weak/commercial, not used for figures.

### Inferences
- Test fee plus coaching can reach BDT 40,000–50,000, well above plausible budgets for segment (c); pricing/installments must be tied to the test the segment actually needs.

### Gaps
- No income data by segment (BBS HIES), no EMI/installment norms, no COD/Rokomari data, no 10 Minute School pricing.

## 5. Regulations relevant to marketing

### Takeaway
Digital Commerce Operation Guidelines 2021 set delivery windows and disclosure duties; promotional SMS rules exist but secondary sources conflict or are weak.

### Cited Findings
- Delivery within 5 days (same city) / 10 days (different city); platforms must display price, charges and purchase/return conditions, and cannot erase ratings/reviews; authority can cancel trade licence/VAT registration; buyers can complain to the Directorate of Consumer Rights Protection — [Daily Star overview (2021)](https://www.thedailystar.net/law-our-rights/news/overview-the-digital-commerce-operation-guidelines-2021-2128871); the fetched summary did not cover refund rules
- Promotional SMS: non-masking numeric sender, expected 9 AM–9 PM BST, filtered against the NDNC registry; masking generally for transactional messages; all A2P traffic via BTRC-listed aggregator — [Telerivet](https://www.telerivet.com/blog/bangladesh-sms-compliance-btrc-masking). WEAK SOURCE: vendor blog, hedged language, internal date inconsistencies, no primary BTRC citation; verify with BTRC.
- Telcos have been fined over promotional SMS limits and subscribers can dial codes to stop promo SMS — [Developing Telecoms](https://developingtelecoms.com/telecom-business/telecom-regulation/17490-bangladesh-telcos-fined-over-promotional-sms-limits.html), [TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/237061) (headlines/snippets only)

### Inferences
- Avoid "guaranteed band" claims and show refund terms prominently; treat as legal-review items.

### Gaps
- Not found: Consumer Rights Protection Act 2009 advertising provisions, data protection/consent law status (Personal Data Protection Ordinance/Cyber Security Ordinance 2025), BTRC primary documents.
